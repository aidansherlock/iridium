Follow one price all the way down

A worked example with made-up numbers. Real edges are small.

Step 1 of 6

  1. Read the sharpest price

    Pinnacle has the Yankees at -150 and the Red Sox at +135. A price is a probability in disguise: 60.0% and 42.6%. Together that is 102.6%. The extra 2.6% is the book's margin.

  2. Take the margin out

    True chances add to 100%, so the margin comes off. What is left, 58.9% and 41.1%, is the reference every other number is measured against.

  3. Compare venues on the same contract

    Your book offers +104, which implies 49.0%. A prediction market shows the same side at 52¢. Same game, same side, same line, same settlement rules. If any of those differ, the prices are shown apart and never compared.

  4. Add the fee, at your size

    The market charges its fee once per order. 100 contracts at 52¢ cost $52.00 plus $1.75, so the real price is about 53.8¢ and the edge falls from 6.9 points to about 5.1. Fees eat most small edges, which is why they are counted.

  5. Size it, then cap it

    Full Kelly says 19.4% of bankroll. Iridium takes a quarter of that, then caps any single bet at 3%. On $1,000 that is $30. A new desk starts at half that size.

  6. Refuse to guess

    A stake only shows next to a fresh, verified price. Past the freshness limit, 30 minutes by default, the row turns stale and the stake goes to zero. The same happens with an unknown fee, too little depth, or a game that has started.

One loop, from finding a price to grading it

Inside the app it is called the Lens. Filters, your place in the list and the row you are inspecting survive every refresh.

  1. 1

    Discover

    The board lists one exact contract per row: event, market, side and signed line.

  2. 2

    Inspect

    A panel opens beside the list with the decision: price, reference chance, net EV after fees and stake.

  3. 3

    Compare

    Every venue is priced through the same engine and laid out on a ladder and a price ruler.

  4. 4

    Review risk

    Exposure before and after this stake, which cap binds, and how fresh the inputs are.

  5. 5

    Record

    You place the bet yourself, then record it. Iridium does not place it for you.

  6. 6

    Evaluate

    Each recorded bet is graded against the Pinnacle close, on the exact same contract.

Seven screens, one desk

A league bar sits on every screen. Deep links work everywhere, so a row, a tab and a filter can be shared as one address.

HomeOpportunitiesMarketsResearchPerformanceJournalSettings

Home

Your desk at a glance: this month's profit and loss and drawdown, today's best playable contracts, the games starting soonest, and your recent bets.

Opportunities

The decision workspace. One exact contract per row with the venue price, the reference chance, net EV after fees, available size, a suggested stake, and a status that is checked again every time the board is read.

Markets

Every Pinnacle move, scored 0 to 100. The movement score describes how big, fast and clean a move was. It is not a probability, and the screen says so.

Research

All of today's games, signal or not. Every book on every side, records over the last 5, 10, 20 and 50 games, players, lineups and history.

Performance

Six scopes that are never blended: signal grading, paper orders, your bets, model calibration, execution and portfolio.

Journal

What you actually bet. Profit and loss, ROI, win rate with a range, drawdown and streak. Every edit and settlement is audited, and it exports to CSV.

Settings

Bankroll, unit, your books, the Kelly fraction and exposure limits. Your data key and what a scan costs. Backup, diagnostics and a health check.

For investors: built, measured, and still a guess

These are kept apart on purpose. Nothing on this page is a return, a hit rate or a user count.

The problem: recreational bettors are shown prices that quietly give the edge back. A line with no size behind it. A prediction-market contract whose fee and tie rule change the payoff. A closing-line number that compares two different lines. Most tools show stats or an EV figure. Few show the fee-adjusted cost at a real size, and fewer grade their own recommendations in a way the user can audit.

Built

In the 0.10.0 source today

  • One exact contract per row. Quotes on another line or tie rule are never compared.
  • Tie and push rules mapped to cash flows. An unknown settlement rule blocks the stake.
  • Exposure in three scopes (actual, paper, planned) that are never added together.
  • Calibration on a frozen, versioned holdout. Repeated scans cannot inflate the sample.
  • Paper replay in time order: a later result cannot change an earlier allocation.
  • Watches on an exact contract that fire once and never place an order.
  • Python standard library only, SQLite, and a server that listens on your machine alone.

Hypotheses

Not yet tested

  • Serious recreational bettors will pay for honest, fee-aware comparison across sportsbooks and prediction markets more readily than for picks.
  • Recording decisions and grading them against the close builds trust faster than marketing a win rate.
  • Sports liquidity on prediction markets keeps growing, so depth-aware pricing matters more each season.
  • A small beta will surface the data and settlement problems that matter before a hosted version is built.

What it is not

Stated plainly

  • Iridium does not place bets, promise returns, or hold a licence to offer wagering.
  • Not placing bets is a product decision. It is not a regulatory assurance.
  • The regulatory and data-licensing position for a hosted version is an open item.
  • Market data is licensed from its providers, displayed and derived from, never resold.
